01 — The Stunt

Skyhook.

I'm standing up in the back of a moving convertible. A plane comes in low overhead trailing a rope. I catch it, clip it into my harness, and it rips me straight up out of the car — the car keeps driving without me. The plane carries me up to altitude, and then I cut away from the rope, freefall, and open my parachute.

Car to plane to skydive, in one continuous take.

02 — The Film

We don’t shoot a stunt. We shoot a Bond opening.

The extraction is the spectacle. But a spectacle on its own gets watched once. Wrapped in ninety seconds of story it gets watched three times and sent to a friend. So we build the whole thing as a cold open: a man in a suit is running from something. Motorcycle, briefcase, a car full of people screaming at him to get in — and then the sky takes him.

The turn
The car isn’t the escape. The plane is.

For sixty seconds the audience thinks they’re watching a getaway — bike to car, chase grammar they’ve seen a hundred times. Then a plane comes down and takes him out of the car, and the getaway car keeps driving without him. We never show what he was running from. The absence is what makes it play as a cold open instead of a stunt clip.

I The escape

Empty airfield. Engine before image. A motorcycle tears into frame — a man in a suit and helmet, a parachute already on his back, a 1win briefcase in his hand. He glances back over his shoulder at something we never see, brakes hard, and is off the bike before it’s settled.

II The getaway

A 1win-branded convertible is running, people inside waving him over — go, go, go. He throws the briefcase in ahead of him, jumps in, and the car peels off, leaving the bike abandoned in frame. At speed he clips into the tow rigging and stands up in the back.

III The extraction

The plane comes in low, trailing a rope. He catches it, clips in, and the line goes tight. He is gone — and the car keeps driving without him. That empty car, still accelerating down an open runway, is the single frame this whole film is built to deliver.

IV The exit

The plane carries him up. At altitude he cuts away from the rope, freefalls in the suit, and opens the parachute he’s been wearing since the first shot. He lands, walks out of frame, doesn’t look back. Title card: 1win.

The shot-by-shot sequence and camera plan are already written and go to the stunt company’s film crew ahead of the build day. Acts I and II are a chase — reshootable as many times as we need. Act III happens exactly once, and it is covered from seven camera positions.

03 — The Production

This is not me and a GoPro.

The aerial extraction is run by a professional stunt and aviation outfit that does this for a living. They supply the aircraft, the pilot, the rigging, the safety crew and the film team, and they train us on the ground before anyone leaves it. That side of the project is already agreed and booked — it is not something we’re figuring out on the day.

Who runs it

The operator

  • Aircraft, pilot and extraction rigging
  • Stunt coordinator and safety crew
  • Ground training before the build day
  • Vehicles, permits and insurance
Who shoots it

The film team

  • Cinema camera on the ground and in the car
  • Drone coverage of the run and the extraction
  • Camera in the aircraft, shooting down the rope
  • Long lens locked wide on the extraction itself
What I bring

My own package

  • Insta360 on the helmet for the POV
  • Cameras rigged inside the vehicle
  • The full shot list, written before we arrive
  • All of the editing, start to finish
How we protect the shot
Acts one and two can be reshot. The extraction happens once.

The motorcycle, the run and the getaway we can shoot as many times as we want. The moment the plane takes me out of the car is a single take, so it is covered from seven camera positions at the same time — and the crew are briefed on exactly which frame matters before anyone rolls.

04 — Branding

On me. In my hand. Under me.

Because it’s built as a film rather than a single stunt clip, the brand isn’t a logo dropped on top of the action — it’s carried by the wardrobe, the prop and the vehicle the story is actually about.

On me

The wardrobe

  • Black suit, Bond wardrobe, worn road to landing
  • 1win shirt and cuffs, reading at the collar and wrist all film
  • 1win on the helmet
  • Still on me through the freefall
In my hand

The briefcase

  • 1win mark on the case, in hand through the escape
  • Held in close-up as he runs for the car
  • Thrown into the branded car — two brand surfaces in one frame
Under me

The car

  • 1win branding across a convertible
  • On screen for the whole second and third act
  • Holds the frame alone after the extraction — the closing image is a 1win car driving on without me
